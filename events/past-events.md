### 2026-10-09 — Mw 7.7 Azuero/Herrera earthquake → state of emergency

- **Type:** earthquake (shallow strike-slip, Azuero-Soná fault system)
- **Location:** epicentre 12 km WSW of Pitaloza Arriba, southern Herrera Province (7.587°N, 80.769°W; the Panamanian Institute of Geosciences placed it ~39 km NW of Tonosí, Los Santos); felt nationwide and in Costa Rica and Colombia
- **Severity / scale:** USGS Mw 7.7, depth 12.6 km, max intensity MMI IX (Violent); the Panamanian Institute of Geosciences later revised to M7.6 (official balance uses 7.5), depth ~4 km. 900+ aftershocks recorded (becoming less frequent), largest Mw 6.6 (20:25 UTC). A Pacific tsunami warning (Panama, Colombia, Ecuador, Nicaragua, Guatemala) was issued, then lifted.
- **Casualties & damage:** 3 dead, 27 injured (a third death — an elderly woman hospitalised since 14 Oct — was confirmed by CSS on 30 Oct; the second death was a woman who suffered a head injury during an evacuation and died in Santiago de Veraguas hospital on 22 Oct). 52,874 people affected (EDAN, 27 Oct; incl. 160 pregnant women, 2,143 with disabilities). Damage by 20 Oct: 2,318 homes totally damaged, 5,546 partial, 1,260 minor (16,302 homes assessed by 27 Oct); 141 shelters housing 4,501 people (mostly Veraguas); 619 schools and 120 health facilities affected (13 out of service); 127 public buildings, 44 churches, 66 road sections and 22 bridges (SINAPROC, 27 Oct); ~2,300 landslides and a major ground fracture at Isla Cañas. Tonosí lost water and power; the Pedasí–Tonosí road and Oria River bridge were closed; six communities were cut off; ~300 people evacuated from Isla de Cañas. Tocumen airport and the Panama City metro were suspended temporarily; the Canal kept operating (minor damage to four office buildings).
- **What happened:** On 9 Oct 2026 at 12:56 local time a magnitude-7.7 quake struck the Azuero Peninsula, toppling buildings and roads across the centre of the country. The government declared a state of emergency the following day (still in force as of 28 Oct); President Mulino ordered immediate repairs of roads, hospitals, schools and homes, prioritising the most vulnerable. The US pledged $5m and sent a disaster-response team and helicopters with food. By 28 Oct SINAPROC had moved 208,317 lb of aid by air, 365 tonnes by road and 60,193 lb by sea to 175 communities, with ~19,000 personnel deployed and 38 medical air evacuations; yellow rain/landslide alerts remained in place over Tonosí and parts of Veraguas. Classes were cancelled and the national earthquake-evacuation drill (due three days later) was called off; schools in Los Santos, Herrera, Coclé and Veraguas remained closed as of 28 Oct.
- **Lessons for preparedness:** strict building codes spared the capital's high-rises, but shallow inland quakes on the Azuero-Soná fault cut off communities (roads, water, power) far from the epicentre; aftershocks drove people to sleep outdoors for days, and heavy rain on damaged slopes then triggered landslides; a tsunami warning requires a practised coastal-evacuation reflex even when the threat recedes.

### 2023 — Cobre Panamá mine protests and closure

- **Type:** civil unrest / environmental / economic
- **Location:** nationwide protests and road blockades; the mine itself in Donoso, Colón; port blockade at Punta Rincón
- **Severity / scale:** one of the largest protest movements in Panama's recent history; mine closed and still shuttered as of late 2026
- **Casualties & damage:** economic shock — the mine was ~5% of GDP and ~75% of goods exports; ~40,800 direct/indirect jobs. Growth fell from 7.4% (2023) to ~2.9% (2024); export losses ~$2bn (CONEP); First Quantum estimates $3.5bn lost economic contribution over 2024–25.
- **What happened:** On 20 Oct 2023 the government enacted Law 406, approving a revised 20-year concession for First Quantum's Minera Panamá. Mass protests and blockades followed. On 28 Nov 2023 the Supreme Court ruled unanimously that Law 406 was unconstitutional; President Cortizo ordered an orderly, safe closure the same day. In 2026 the government allowed processing of 38m tonnes of stockpiled ore to prevent environmental risks (acid rock drainage), and the mining chamber CAMIPA recommended resuming operations; a definitive government decision on the mine's future is expected late 2026.
- **Lessons for preparedness:** a single asset concentrated ~5% of GDP and ~75% of exports, so its loss hit growth and public finances in one year; blockades cut roads and supply chains nationwide; the dispute remains unresolved, so renewed unrest is plausible.

### 2020–2023 — COVID-19 pandemic

- **Type:** epidemic / public-health emergency
- **Location:** nationwide
- **Severity / scale:** over 1 million confirmed cases; ~8,600 deaths over three years (case-fatality ~0.83%)
- **Casualties & damage:** 1,032,316 cases and 8,621 deaths over three years (peer-reviewed); deep economic contraction during 2020–21 lockdowns
- **What happened:** First case confirmed 9 Mar 2020; state of emergency declared 13 Mar 2020. Panama imposed one of the strictest lockdowns in the region, including a gender-based quarantine (women out Mon/Wed/Fri, men Tue/Thu/Sat, no one on Sundays) so police could spot violators at a glance, plus heavy fines and a suspension of international flights. The gender rule drew criticism for harassing transgender and non-binary people.
- **Lessons for preparedness:** lockdowns are enforceable but socially costly; the gender-day system was a novel but problematic control; pandemic readiness needs stockpiled medical supplies, remote-school/work capacity, and clear, non-discriminatory rules from day one.
- **Sources:**

### 2023–2024 — Panama Canal drought and transit restrictions

- **Type:** drought / water crisis (economic)
- **Location:** Canal watershed (Gatún Lake) and nationwide water supply
- **Severity / scale:** transits cut from ~38 to 24/day (late 2023); driest October since 1950 (43% below average); Gatún Lake at a record low on 1 Jan 2024
- **Casualties & damage:** no direct casualties; major economic and supply-chain disruption (4,000+ fewer ship transits projected for 2024). Gatún Lake also supplies drinking water to Panama City and Colón.
- **What happened:** A prolonged El Niño-driven drought beginning early 2023 drained Gatún Lake, forcing the Canal Authority to cut daily transits and impose draft limits. Conditions recovered through 2025 (La Niña rainfall, ~36 transits/day, 50-ft draft), but a new El Niño returned in 2026.
- **Lessons for preparedness:** the Canal is both a shipping artery and a drinking-water source, so drought hits both global trade and local water security; water conservation and reservoir management are core national resilience issues.

  - Reuters (2026 transit caps): https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/panama-canal-cap-daily-transits-anticipating-severe-el-nio-2026-08-20/
  - gCaptain (recovery and El Niño watch): https://gcaptain.com/el-nino-watch-puts-panama-canal-back-in-focus-after-dramatic-drought-recovery/

### 2024–2025 — Recent moderate earthquakes (Chiriquí region)

- 2024-07-08: M5.3, Chiriquí (early morning). 24 homes affected (18 David, 4 Bugaba, 1 Boquete, 1 Boquerón); 15 notable aftershocks (M3.0–4.4). [SINAPROC](https://www.mingob.gob.pa/autoridades-brindan-balance-sobre-las-afectaciones-por-el-sismo/)
- 2024-07-22: M5.7, near Puerto Armuelles (Panama–Costa Rica border), depth 3 km; no deaths reported. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/earthquake-magnitude-57-strikes-panama-costa-rica-border-region-gfz-reports-2024-07-22/)
- 2025-03-21: M6.2, ~123 km SSE of Burica, depth 10 km; felt but no damage reported. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/panama-earthquake-pacific-2b7a1527b8ce18ba376c119814afc65d)
- 2025-07-14: M6.2 (preliminary), ~210 km south of Punta Burica; no damage reported. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/panama-earthquake-chiriqui-8a01c90862d7b9ccfbb65ab24014e53f)

> Pattern: western Panama (Chiriquí, near the Costa Rica border) sees frequent moderate seismicity; the 2026 Azuero event was a much larger shallow inland quake on a different fault system.

### 2025 — Cyber incidents (ransomware / hacks)

- **Type:** cyber
- **Location:** Ministry of Economy & Finance (MEF); Ministry of Health (MINSA); Electoral Tribunal (election-day attacks)
- **Severity / scale:** MEF breach claimed by INC Ransom gang (1.5 TB allegedly stolen); MINSA platform unauthorised access; 31.5 million cyberattacks against the Electoral Tribunal on the 5 May 2024 general-election day (all blocked)
- **Casualties & damage:** no physical casualties; data-exposure and trust damage, contained per government statements
- **What happened:** Sep 2025 — MEF disclosed a possible malware compromise on a workstation (contained, core systems unaffected) after INC Ransom claimed the attack; MINSA disclosed a weekend hack of a public planning platform (no patient data). The Electoral Tribunal reported blocking 31.5m attacks during the 2024 election (down from 90m in 2019).
- **Lessons for preparedness:** Panama's critical institutions are already under sustained cyber pressure; see the cyberattack scenario in `future-events.md`.
