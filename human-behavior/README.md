## Key takeaways (the short version)

- **People almost never panic; most are passive.** Leach's 10–80–10: ~10% act effectively, ~80% are stunned/mechanical, ~10% are counterproductive. Paralysis and under-reaction — not stampedes — are the real risks.
- **Disasters bring out cooperation, not chaos.** Mutual aid and the "therapeutic community" dominate; looting and antisocial behavior are rare and require specific conditions (near-total destruction + pre-existing inequality + failed authority).
- **Starvation has a predictable psychology.** Food obsession, apathy, irritability, depression, social withdrawal — documented by the Minnesota Starvation Experiment and visible in Leningrad accounts. Refeeding is itself a long, hard phase.
- **Meaning and social bonds predict survival** — more than physical strength (Frankl, Gonzales, Ripley). People survive *for* someone or something.
- **Extreme privation degrades ordinary morality.** Survival can favor the compromised, not the noble (Levi's "grey zone").
- **Preparation converts "stunned" into "acting."** Rehearsal and information are the most valuable things in a crisis (Ripley).
