# Budget tiers (Panama)

Three example budgets using the real Oct-2026 prices elsewhere in this folder. They are illustrative — prices fluctuate and family size matters. The point: **you do not need money to be meaningfully prepared; you need to start and build gradually.** Add one item per shopping trip.

Family of 4, for reference.

## Tier 1 — "Básico" (3 days) — ~$53

The floor; covers the 72-hour window.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Garrafón 5-gal water refill | $4.99 |
| Rice 5 lb + beans 2 lb + tuna 4 + sardines 4 + pasta 2 + oil 1.5 L + sugar + salt + oatmeal + powdered milk | ~$25.50 |
| First aid: 22-pc kit + acetaminophen + suero oral ×2 + curitas | ~$10.50 |
| Flashlight + matches + 1 candle + whistle + duct tape | ~$11.40 |
| Plastic bags for documents | ~$1.00 |
| **Total** | **~$53** |

## Tier 3 — "Mejorado" (14 days) — ~$331

Adds a two-week reserve and water purification.

| Add on top of Tier 2 | Cost |
|---|---|
| Water for 14 days (~23 garrafones total) | $114.77 |
| Food to 14 days | $25.50 |
| Unscented bleach (purification) | $3.66 |
| **Total (incl. Tier 2)** | **~$331** |

(Not priced: a generator or solar panel — useful for long outages but a large single purchase; add only after the basics.)

## Cash reserve (separate from the kits)

Keep a small amount of **cash in USD small bills** at home — in the cyberattack scenario (the #1 future risk), cards, ATMs and transfers can fail for days.

## The rule

Start at Tier 1 this week (~$53). Move to Tier 2 over the next month or two. Tier 3 is the long-term goal. Preparedness is a habit, not a shopping trip.
