# Food reserves (Panama)

Build your food reserve from the same staples Panamanians already eat — cheaper and nothing goes to waste. The government's own **Canasta Básica Familiar de Alimentos (CBFA)** is the benchmark.

## The canasta básica (what "enough to eat" costs)

- Official MEF basket: **59 products in 10 groups** (meats/fish, cereals, vegetables, beans, fruits, fats, dairy, eggs, sugar, misc.), built to minimum calorie needs.
- Official monthly cost (June 2026): **B/.366.98** for Panamá & San Miguelito districts; **B/.345.20** resto urbano.
- Acodeco (2nd half Sept 2026): the same basket cost **$306.03 in supermarkets** vs **$360.79 in minisúper/abarroterías** — a **$54.76 gap**. **Buy at supermarkets, not the corner store**, if you can.

## The reserve (quantities per person)

Aim for shelf-stable, no-cook-or-easy-cook food. SINAPROC stresses "canned, sometimes dehydrated, easy to open and prepare" and watching expiry dates.

- **3 days:** the minimum (matches the 72-hour kit).
- **7 days:** a realistic goal.
- **14 days:** for the main scenarios (earthquake recovery, drought rationing, civil-unrest road closures).

Per person per day, roughly: ~0.5 lb rice/pasta + ~0.25 lb beans/lentils + 1 can protein (tuna/sardines) + oil/salt/sugar for seasoning + powdered milk or oatmeal for breakfast. Multiply by days and people.

## Storage in a tropical climate

- Heat and humidity spoil food fast: keep dry goods in sealed containers/jars, off the floor, away from sun.
- Rotate stock — eat and replace so nothing expires (SINAPROC's advice).
- Prefer easy-open (pull-tab) cans so no can opener is needed in a crisis.

## Low-cost tip

Rice + beans + sardines/tuna + a little oil and salt is the cheapest complete emergency food in Panama. A 7-day reserve for one adult from the staples above costs roughly **$10–15**.
